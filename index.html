What changed this month — for the growth lead

  • Win rate by value is 41%, by count 29%. The gap says large pursuits are won and small ones are lost: effort is spread across too many low-odds bids.
  • Hospitality and tourism pursuits have slipped — four RFP dates moved right. Transport, aviation and defence-related buildings are holding.
  • Top loss reason for three quarters: price / fee level, then "positioned late". Both are decided before the RFP lands, at Go/No-Go.
  • Decisions needed: No-Go on two pursuits below threshold; Red team date for the airport terminal package; close four overdue debriefs.

Eight patterns a win/loss file should answer

Each chart below is the view I would build from the pursuit record in the first 60 days. The numbers are invented to show the shape of the analysis; the real patterns may differ.

1 · Win rate by fee size

So what: 42% of bids are under USD 5m and win 18% of the time. Small bids need a lighter process or a higher bar at Go/No-Go.

2 · Win rate by when we first met the client

So what: Bids where the first meeting came after the RFP win about one in nine. This is the strongest single Go/No-Go test.

3 · Win rate by number of competitors

So what: Each extra bidder costs about seven points. With awards down and fields growing, field size belongs on the scorecard.

4 · Win rate by sector and country (by value)

So what: Strength is transport, aviation and defence in the UAE. Hospitality in Saudi Arabia is the weakest cell and still takes bid effort.

5 · Pursuit mix by quarter (live fee value, USD m)

So what: The mix is moving toward infrastructure-led buildings, but hospitality and residential still takes 30% of pursuit value.

6 · Win rate by colour reviews held

So what: Proposals with both reviews win at nearly three times the rate of those with none. Review dates should be booked at Go.

7 · Does the Go/No-Go score predict the result?

So what: If predicted and actual track each other, the score can be trusted for decisions. If not, the weights are refitted.

8 · Bid cost per USD 1m of fee won (USD k)

So what: Winning the same fee costs a third more than a year ago. Selectivity, reuse of content and AI-assisted drafting are the levers.

Go / No-Go — score the pursuit, see the recommendation

Method: five factors scored 1–5 and weighted (relationship 25%, positioning 25%, fit 20%, team 15%, terms 15%), mapped to a win probability between 5% and 75%, then reduced for field size. Weights are a starting point; in use they are fitted to the firm's own win/loss history each quarter. Approval level follows a delegation-of-authority table by fee value and risk score; the bands shown are placeholders.
